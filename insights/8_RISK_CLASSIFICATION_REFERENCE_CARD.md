# 🎯 RISK CLASSIFICATION REFERENCE CARD
## Comprehensive Guide to Mental Health Risk Levels

---

## 📊 THREE RISK LEVELS DEFINED

### **🔴 HIGH RISK - IMMEDIATE ATTENTION REQUIRED**

#### **Clinical Criteria**:
- **Depression Score**: 25-30 (Severe range)
- **Anxiety Score**: 18-21 (Severe range)  
- **Stress Level**: 8-10 (Extremely high)
- **Sleep Hours**: <5 hours (Severely inadequate)
- **Social Support**: <40 (Critically low)

#### **Characteristic Profile**:
```
Typical HIGH RISK Individual:
Age: 18-35 years (peak risk period)
Gender: Any (slightly higher in females)
Employment: Student or high-pressure job
Work Environment: On-site or hybrid stressful settings
Mental Health History: Often present but untreated
Current Treatment: Usually not seeking help despite severe symptoms
```

#### **Warning Signs**:
✅ Multiple severe symptoms present simultaneously
✅ Functional impairment evident (work/school performance declining)
✅ Coping mechanisms failing or maladaptive
✅ Social withdrawal or isolation increasing
✅ Thoughts of self-harm or hopelessness may be present
✅ Sleep disturbance significantly impacting daily life

#### **Immediate Actions Required**:
- **Within 24-48 hours**: Professional assessment
- **Within 1 week**: Treatment initiation
- **Ongoing**: Weekly monitoring and support
- **Safety**: Crisis intervention protocols activated

#### **Example Case**:
```
28-year-old female graduate student
Depression: 28/30, Anxiety: 20/21
Sleep: 4.2 hours, Stress: 9/10
Social Support: 32/100
Productivity: 45% (significantly impaired)

RISK ASSESSMENT: HIGH
CONFIDENCE: 92%
REASONING: Severe symptoms across multiple domains with 
critical functional impairment and inadequate coping resources.
```

---

### **🟡 MEDIUM RISK - MONITORING AND EARLY INTERVENTION**

#### **Clinical Criteria**:
- **Depression Score**: 15-24 (Moderate range)
- **Anxiety Score**: 11-17 (Moderate range)
- **Stress Level**: 5-7 (Moderate to high)
- **Sleep Hours**: 5-7 hours (Suboptimal)
- **Social Support**: 40-75 (Adequate but improvable)

#### **Characteristic Profile**:
```
Typical MEDIUM RISK Individual:
Age: 25-50 years (working adult population)
Gender: Mixed distribution
Employment: Various settings with moderate demands
Work Environment: Mixed conditions
Mental Health History: May or may not be present
Current Treatment: Variable - some seeking help, others not
```

#### **Concern Indicators**:
✅ Noticeable symptoms affecting quality of life
✅ Some functional impact but basic responsibilities maintained
✅ Adequate coping strategies but showing strain
✅ Social connections present but not optimally supportive
✅ Occasional stress overload episodes
✅ Beginning to recognize need for support

#### **Recommended Timeline**:
- **Within 2 weeks**: Professional screening
- **Within 1 month**: Treatment planning if indicated
- **Ongoing**: Bi-weekly check-ins
- **Prevention**: Early intervention strategies

#### **Example Case**:
```
42-year-old male software engineer
Depression: 19/30, Anxiety: 14/21
Sleep: 6.3 hours, Stress: 6/10
Social Support: 65/100
Productivity: 72% (moderately affected)

RISK ASSESSMENT: MEDIUM
CONFIDENCE: 84%
REASONING: Moderate symptoms with preserved functioning and 
adequate support systems, but trending toward higher risk without intervention.
```

---

### **🟢 LOW RISK - ROUTINE MONITORING**

#### **Clinical Criteria**:
- **Depression Score**: 0-14 (Minimal to mild range)
- **Anxiety Score**: 0-10 (Minimal to mild range)
- **Stress Level**: 1-4 (Low to moderate)
- **Sleep Hours**: 7-9 hours (Optimal)
- **Social Support**: 75-100 (Strong network)

#### **Characteristic Profile**:
```
Typical LOW RISK Individual:
Age: 30-65 years (stable adult period)
Gender: Any (often shows good self-care practices)
Employment: Stable positions with reasonable demands
Work Environment: Generally supportive or flexible
Mental Health History: Typically minimal or well-managed
Current Treatment: May have preventive care or past successful treatment
```

#### **Positive Indicators**:
✅ Minimal to no concerning symptoms
✅ Excellent daily functioning and productivity
✅ Strong, supportive social connections
✅ Effective stress management and coping skills
✅ Healthy lifestyle practices maintained
✅ Good self-awareness and help-seeking when needed

#### **Monitoring Schedule**:
- **Quarterly**: Routine wellness checks
- **Annual**: Comprehensive mental health screening
- **As needed**: Based on life changes or stressors
- **Prevention**: Maintenance of healthy habits

#### **Example Case**:
```
55-year-old non-binary consultant
Depression: 4/30, Anxiety: 2/21
Sleep: 7.8 hours, Stress: 3/10
Social Support: 88/100
Productivity: 94% (excellent performance)

RISK ASSESSMENT: LOW
CONFIDENCE: 95%
REASONING: Minimal symptoms with excellent protective factors, 
strong functioning, and robust support systems indicating 
good mental health resilience.
```

## 🔄 RISK TRANSITION PATTERNS

### **Progression Indicators** (LOW → MEDIUM → HIGH):
1. **Sleep deterioration**: From 8+ hours to <6 hours
2. **Stress accumulation**: Gradual increase over months
3. **Social withdrawal**: Decreasing support network engagement
4. **Performance decline**: Noticeable drops in productivity
5. **Coping strategy failure**: Reliance on unhealthy mechanisms
6. **Symptom escalation**: Gradual increase in depression/anxiety scores

### **Improvement Indicators** (HIGH → MEDIUM → LOW):
1. **Sleep improvement**: Restoring adequate sleep duration
2. **Stress reduction**: Implementing effective management techniques
3. **Social reconnection**: Strengthening support networks
4. **Performance recovery**: Returning to baseline functioning
5. **Healthy coping**: Adopting constructive stress management
6. **Symptom reduction**: Decreasing depression/anxiety scores

## 📋 DETAILED RISK ASSESSMENT CHECKLIST

### **HIGH RISK Red Flags** (3+ present):
- [ ] Depression score ≥25 or anxiety score ≥18
- [ ] Sleep <5 hours consistently
- [ ] Stress level ≥8
- [ ] Social support <40
- [ ] Productivity <60%
- [ ] Functional impairment in work/school
- [ ] Withdrawal from social activities
- [ ] Expressing hopelessness or despair
- [ ] Neglecting self-care
- [ ] Substance use as coping mechanism

### **MEDIUM RISK Warning Signs** (2-3 present):
- [ ] Depression score 15-24 or anxiety score 11-17
- [ ] Sleep 5-7 hours
- [ ] Stress level 5-7
- [ ] Social support 40-75
- [ ] Productivity 60-80%
- [ ] Occasional difficulty coping
- [ ] Mild social withdrawal
- [ ] Some self-care neglect
- [ ] Beginning to recognize problems

### **LOW RISK Protective Factors** (5+ present):
- [ ] Depression score <15 and anxiety score <11
- [ ] Sleep 7-9 hours
- [ ] Stress level <5
- [ ] Social support >75
- [ ] Productivity >80%
- [ ] Excellent daily functioning
- [ ] Strong social connections
- [ ] Effective coping strategies
- [ ] Consistent self-care practices
- [ ] Good help-seeking behavior

## 🎯 INTERVENTION MATCHING BY RISK LEVEL

### **HIGH RISK Interventions**:
- **Immediate**: Crisis counseling, emergency services
- **Short-term**: Intensive outpatient programs, medication evaluation
- **Medium-term**: Cognitive behavioral therapy, support groups
- **Long-term**: Ongoing therapy, lifestyle restructuring, relapse prevention

### **MEDIUM RISK Interventions**:
- **Immediate**: Screening and brief intervention
- **Short-term**: Counseling, stress management workshops
- **Medium-term**: Therapy when indicated, skills training
- **Long-term**: Prevention programs, maintenance strategies

### **LOW RISK Maintenance**:
- **Routine**: Annual screening, wellness promotion
- **Prevention**: Education, stress management resources
- **Support**: Peer support networks, community resources
- **Optimization**: Performance enhancement, resilience building

## ⚠️ SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

### **High-Risk Subpopulations**:
- **Postpartum individuals**: Hormonal changes increase vulnerability
- **LGBTQ+ community**: Minority stress and discrimination effects
- **Healthcare workers**: Burnout and trauma exposure
- **First responders**: Chronic stress and shift work impacts
- **Students**: Academic pressure and transition stress
- **Older adults**: Isolation and health-related stress

### **Cultural Sensitivity Notes**:
- **Expression variations**: Different cultures express distress differently
- **Help-seeking patterns**: Vary significantly across cultural groups
- **Family involvement**: Importance differs by cultural background
- **Stigma levels**: Cultural attitudes toward mental health vary widely
